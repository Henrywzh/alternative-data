## Pair implication

The long-Spring / short-Juneyao case is not just earnings divergence; the
valuation asymmetry reinforces it:

* Spring: price implies a 42% beat.  Our model (annualised ~3.69 EPS)
  supports a beat larger than that, so the stock is not yet fully
  repriced on our view - room remains.
* Juneyao: price implies +93% vs consensus and +70% vs our model.  If the
  H1-2026 print confirms our view (yield conversion weaker, fleet flat),
  Juneyao faces both an earnings miss AND multiple compression.

The pair payoff is asymmetric: Spring's upside is partially priced but our
view exceeds it; Juneyao's downside is fully mispriced on our numbers.

## Limitations

* EV/EBITDAR is incomplete (Spring/Juneyao debt/depreciation gaps labelled);
  leases are estimated from the unit-economics aircraft CASK where needed.
* Model FY26 EPS is a crude H1 x 2 annualisation (H2 is seasonally stronger
  for mainland LCCs, so this understates the annual view).
* Valuation is a diagnostic snapshot, not a target price; no trade approval.
